earth-terrain.bin

GEBCO Compilation Group (2026), GEBCO_2026 Grid.
https://www.gebco.net/data-products-gridded-bathymetry-data/gebco2026-grid
COG distribution by the Australian Antarctic Division:
https://source.coop/ausantarctic/gebco
https://data.source.coop/ausantarctic/gebco/GEBCO_2026.tif

Visual overview: global geographic grid, 4096 x 2048 (~9.8 km at the equator).
Prepared with rasterio average resampling from available source overviews.
Stored elevation = R * 256 + G - 32768 metres, north-to-south rows.
Blue encodes signed distance to the connected ocean boundary in source pixels:
distance = (B - 128) / 4, positive inland, saturated at +/-31.75 pixels.
Ocean membership is the largest connected negative-elevation component, joined
across the dateline. Enclosed negative basins do not become ocean. This is an
approximate coastline derived from the overview, not an exact shoreline survey.
RGB values are data, not a visual color ramp. Elevation PNG round-trip checked.
The runtime loads a gzip-compressed numeric copy (ECT1 header, little-endian
uint32 width/height, then RGBA bytes). It does not read elevation through canvas:
browser fingerprinting protection can add noise to canvas readback and corrupt
packed numeric heights. The preparation script writes a reference PNG to exports/earth-elevation.png.
The GPU uses a two-channel float texture (32-bit filtering when supported,
16-bit fallback) and averaged display LODs;
inspection reads the original prepared metre values.
Display mip levels average their polar rows to a single value per pole, and
globe pole vertices share the corresponding native-row average height.
Observed overview elevation range: -10353..6320 m.
This overview supports visualization, not precise local height measurements.
Negative elevations remain available for inspection; geometric oceans are flat
at sea level. The blue outline uses geographic distance to the ocean boundary,
independently of the elevation derivatives used for inland contour lines.
Land geometry uses WGS84 plus exaggerated positive relief; contour labels and
inspected elevations always use unexaggerated overview elevations.
Reproduction: scripts/prepare-terrain.py (temporary rasterio/numpy/Pillow/SciPy env).

USGS Astrogeology Science Center / NASA Mars Viking Global Color Mosaic (925 m/px,

Credit: USGS Astrogeology Science Center / NASA Viking Mission.

Both planetary display DEMs are area-averaged to an 8192 x 4096 global
planetocentric equirectangular grid centered at 0° longitude, isotropically
filtered along polar longitudes to prevent orbital cross-track spokes, and stored
in ECT2 format (16-byte header + Zstd-compressed byte-shuffled 2D-predicted
uint16 signed metres). Run scripts/prepare-planet-terrain.py to reproduce them.

nasa-deep-star-map.webp

Deep Star Maps 2020, NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization
Studio, Ernie Wright. Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC. Star positions, brightness and
colors also use the Hipparcos-2 and Tycho-2 catalogs.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004800/a004851/starmap_2020_8k.exr

Source is the celestial ICRF/J2000 plate carrée map. It is centered at
0 h right ascension, with right ascension increasing toward the left and
declination increasing toward the top. The display adaptation compresses
linear brightness by mapping the peak RGB channel p to
0.108 * p^0.48 + 0.10 * p^2 and scaling the channels together. It then averages
2 × 2 pixels in linear light. For the diffuse sky, use a mask based on the
resampled peak RGB channel p: smoothstep(0.003, 0.009, p) multiplied by
(1 - smoothstep(0.022, 0.048, p)). Blend up to 42% toward Rec. 709 luminance
and reduce brightness by up to 12%, both weighted by that mask. This leaves
highlight colors and the darkest areas alone. The resulting 4096 × 2048 map
is encoded as sRGB WebP. No constellation graphics are included.
